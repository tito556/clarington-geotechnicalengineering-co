The glacial till and limestone bedrock under Clarington create a tricky contrast for any geophysical survey. Seismic tomography cuts through that ambiguity. Velocity contrasts between stiff glacial sediments and fractured shale or limestone are sharp here. A standard borehole gives you a point measurement. This method maps the continuous interface. For infrastructure near the Lake Ontario shoreline, knowing where the bedrock dips or where dissolution features hide is not optional. We often pair it with resistivity testing when groundwater or clay content masks the seismic signal. The result is a layered velocity model that engineers can use directly in foundation design or excavation planning.

A 2D velocity cross-section replaces guesswork with a measured profile of bedrock depth and rock quality beneath Clarington's variable overburden.

Scope of work

NBCC 2020 seismic hazard values for Clarington put Sa(0.2) at 0.37g on firm ground. Site class determination per NBCC Table 4.1.8.4.A requires a measured Vs30 or a conservative default. Refraction tomography delivers that measured value. The field setup uses a 24- or 48-channel seismograph with vertical geophones at 2 to 5-metre spacing. A sledgehammer or weight drop source generates the P-wave and S-wave signals. Processing follows ASTM D5777 for refraction and ASTM D7128 for guided surface waves when MASW is integrated. First-arrival picking, travel-time inversion, and ray-tracing produce a 2D velocity cross-section. The deliverable includes interpreted bedrock surface, ripability zones, and low-velocity anomalies. For deep targets, reflection processing stacks CMP gathers to image stratigraphy below 30 metres. The lab holds ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation for geophysical testing and all field acquisition follows the guidelines of the Canadian Geophysical Society.

Local ground factors

The bedrock surface in Clarington is not a flat horizon. The Lindsay Formation limestone and the underlying Blue Mountain shale have been eroded by post-glacial drainage. Buried valleys filled with soft organic silt and loose sand exist. A drilling program alone can miss a narrow valley by metres. Seismic tomography maps these paleochannels. Velocity inversions, where a stiff layer overlies soft material, degrade the refraction assumption. Joint inversion with MASW surveys resolves this. Karst features in the limestone are another risk. Cavities or solution channels appear as sharp low-velocity zones. Missing a karst feature under a proposed footing leads to sudden settlement. The method also identifies fractured rock zones that affect excavation stability and dewatering requirements.

Common questions

What is the typical cost of a seismic tomography survey in Clarington?

Cost depends on line length and depth target. A single 115-metre refraction line with 24 geophones typically ranges from CA$3,360 to CA$5,200. Longer reflection profiles or multiple intersecting lines range from CA$5,800 to CA$7,830. Each quote includes acquisition, processing, and a signed engineering report.

How long does fieldwork take on a typical Clarington site?

A single refraction line of 115 metres takes about 3 to 4 hours of field time. This includes layout, geophone planting, multiple shot points, and equipment demobilization. A full day can cover 3 to 4 lines if access is clear. Reflection surveys take longer due to higher fold requirements. Processing and reporting add 3 to 5 business days.

Can seismic tomography work in winter conditions in Ontario?

Yes. Frozen ground actually improves geophone coupling and reduces surface wave noise. The field crew uses frost pins and drills for geophone placement. The main constraint is safety during heavy snowfall or extreme cold below -20 degrees C. Winter surveys in Clarington are common for projects with spring construction deadlines.
