The glacial till plains and drumlin fields that define Clarington's landscape create a complex subsurface where clay-rich tills sit abruptly against sandy outwash deposits. This variability, typical of the Oak Ridges Moraine region, means groundwater pathways and bedrock depth can change within the span of a single building lot. Our vertical electrical sounding service addresses this directly, using Schlumberger and Wenner arrays to map stratigraphic transitions and identify low-resistivity zones before excavation begins. In the Courtice and Bowmanville areas, where urban expansion meets rural hydrogeology, we combine test pits for direct observation with resistivity profiles that reveal what lies between boreholes, ensuring no conductive clay lens or perched water table goes undetected during site characterization.

Resistivity contrasts in Clarington's moraine deposits can shift from 30 to 200 ohm-m within 50 meters — a single borehole simply cannot capture that lateral variability.

Scope of work

One mistake we see repeatedly in Clarington is assuming that a single borehole log represents conditions across an entire subdivision. The truth is that the region's interlobate moraine deposits create lateral resistivity contrasts of 30 to 200 ohm-m over distances as short as 50 meters. Our VES surveys use a 400-meter maximum electrode spread with 21 depth levels, resolving saturated silts from dry sands and mapping the bedrock interface to within 10 percent depth accuracy. For projects near Lake Ontario's bluffs, where saline intrusion can accelerate steel corrosion, we integrate resistivity tomography with water chemistry sampling to produce a corrosion potential map that structural engineers use directly in foundation design. Each survey includes apparent resistivity curves, iterative inversion models, and a geoelectric cross-section signed by our senior geophysicist.

Local ground factors

The contrast between Clarington's southern lakeshore zone and its northern moraine uplands is stark from a resistivity standpoint. Near Port Darlington, groundwater conductivity can spike above 1,500 µS/cm due to road salt legacy and natural mineral leaching, compressing the useful resistivity range and requiring careful IP (induced polarization) coupling to separate clay responses from true contaminants. Up in Enniskillen, thick sequences of dense basal till produce resistivity above 80 ohm-m, which sounds ideal until you discover embedded boulder beds that mimic bedrock in the inversion model. Misinterpreting a boulder bed as top-of-rock has led to expensive redesigns on at least two municipal infrastructure projects we've reviewed. Our protocol in these ambiguous zones includes a cross-check with seismic refraction or MASW to verify the velocity contrast, eliminating the ambiguity that VES alone cannot resolve in highly resistive, boulder-rich tills.

Common questions

What depth can a VES survey reach in Clarington's glacial deposits?

With a 400-meter maximum electrode spread, we typically investigate to 80-120 meters below surface. The actual depth penetration depends on the resistivity contrast between the overburden and bedrock. In Clarington's northern till plains, where the overburden is highly resistive, we often achieve deeper penetration than in the southern clay-rich zones near Lake Ontario, where conductive clays attenuate the signal more rapidly. For targets deeper than 120 meters, we can deploy an expanded 600-meter spread or recommend complementary seismic refraction to verify the bedrock interface.

How much does a VES survey cost for a typical Clarington residential lot?

For a standard residential lot in Clarington requiring three to five vertical electrical soundings with full inversion modeling and a stamped geophysical report, budget between CA$970 and CA$1,430. The final cost depends on access conditions, the required depth of investigation, and whether we need to combine VES with IP measurements to resolve conductive clay from saline groundwater. We provide a firm quote after reviewing your geotechnical investigation scope and site plan.

How do you distinguish between clay layers and contaminated groundwater using resistivity?

This is one of the most common interpretation challenges in Clarington because the glaciolacustrine clays in the Bowmanville area and salt-impacted groundwater both produce low resistivity readings below 20 ohm-m. We apply three techniques to separate them: first, we collect induced polarization (IP) measurements alongside resistivity — clays exhibit high chargeability, while dissolved-phase contaminants show low chargeability. Second, we correlate lateral resistivity trends with existing monitoring well data for total dissolved solids. Third, we run a paired spt drilling location at one or two anomalous VES stations to provide direct soil classification, anchoring the geophysical model to physical samples.
